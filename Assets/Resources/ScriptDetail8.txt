ACT 3 SCENE 5

<u>Juliet</u>
Speakest thou from thy heart?

<i><size=75%>Are you speaking from your heart?</size></i>

<u>Nurse</u>
And from my soul too, else beshrew them both.

<i><size=75%>Yes, and from my soul, too, or else a curse on them both!</size></i>

<u>Juliet</u>
Amen!

<i><size=75%>Amen!</size></i>

<u>Nurse</u>
What?

<i><size=75%>What did you say?</size></i>

<u>Juliet</u>
Well, thou hast comforted me marvelous much.
Go in. And tell my lady I am gone,
Having displeased my father, to Laurence' cell
To make confession and to be absolved.

<i><size=75%>Well, you’ve been marvellously comforting. Go inside and tell 
my mother that I’ve gone to Friar Laurence’s cell to 
make confession and be forgiven for my sins after 
displeasing my father like that.</size></i>

<u>Nurse</u>
Marry, I will, and this is wisely done.

<i><size=75%>I will indeed, and I think that’s a smart idea.</size></i>
[Exit]

<u>Juliet</u>
Ancient damnation! O most wicked fiend!
Is it more sin to wish me thus forsworn,
Or to dispraise my lord with that same tongue
Which she hath praised him with above compare
So many thousand times? 

<i><size=75%>Damn old woman! Oh what a wicked devil! Is it more sinful to 
tell me to break my marriage vows or to speak ill of 
Romeo like that when she’s praised him beyond 
comparison so many thousands of times?</size></i> 

Go, counselor;
Thou and my bosom henceforth shall be twain.
I'll to the friar, to know his remedy.
If all else fail, myself have power to die.

<i><size=75%>Go, my former advisor, you and my inner thoughts will be 
separated from now on. I’ll go to the friar to see if 
he has a solution. If all else fails, I still have 
the power to end my own life.</size></i>
[Exit]

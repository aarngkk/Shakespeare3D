ACT 3 SCENE 5

<u>Capulet</u>
When the sun sets, the earth doth drizzle dew;
But for the sunset of my brother's son
It rains downright.

<i><size=75%>When the sun sets there’s a drizzle of dew, but my nephew’s 
death, the sunset of his life, has caused a downpour.</size></i>

How now! A conduit, girl? What, still in tears?
Evermore showering? In one little body
Thou counterfeits a bark, a sea, a wind;

<i><size=75%>What is it now, are you some kind of water pipe, girl? 
What, are you still crying? In your little body there’s 
a storm with a boat, a sea, and a wind.</size></i>

For still thy eyes, which I may call the sea,
Do ebb and flow with tears; the bark thy body is,
Sailing in this salt flood; the winds, thy sighs,
Who, raging with thy tears, and they with them,
Without a sudden calm, will overset
Thy tempest-tossèd body. 

<i><size=75%>Your eyes are like the sea ebbing and flowing with tears, your body 
is like a boat sailing through the tears, and your violent 
sighing is like stormwinds that threaten to overpower your 
body as if it were a ship in a tempest.</size></i> 

How now, wife,
Have you delivered to her our decree?

<i><size=75%>What about it, wife? 
Have you told her about my decision?</size></i>

<u>Lady Capulet</u>
Ay, sir; but she will none, she gives you thanks.
I would the fool were married to her grave!

<i><size=75%>Yes, sir, but she won’t have any of it, she just thanks you. 
I wish the silly girl were married to her own grave!</size></i>

<u>Capulet</u>
Soft, take me with you, take me with you, wife.
How, will she none? Doth she not give us thanks?
Is she not proud? Doth she not count her blest,
Unworthy as she is, that we have wrought
So worthy a gentleman to be her bridegroom?

<i><size=75%>What! Hold on, wife, hold on. What is this? She refuses? 
Isn’t she grateful for us? Isn’t she proud of her family? 
Doesn’t she count herself blessed, unworthy as she is, 
that we found such a worthy gentlemen to be her husband?</size></i>
